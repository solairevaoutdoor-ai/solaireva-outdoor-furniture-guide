# Private Label Furniture Manufacturer: How an OEM Programme Actually Runs

## What you can and cannot private label

Labelling, packaging, a logo on the frame, branded cushions, custom hangtags and a branded carton are all straightforward — no tooling, and usually no extra minimum beyond the container itself.

New frames, new sizes and new moulds are a different conversation. Those need tooling and a volume commitment, and the tooling should stay yours under the agreement. Get that in writing before the first mould is cut.

## Packaging is where private label lives or dies

The carton is the first thing your customer sees and the last thing anyone specs. Ask for the carton test result, the inner protection method, and whether the branded carton is printed in one pass or stickered. Stickered cartons look exactly like stickered cartons.

Settle the artwork approval flow up front — who signs off, how many rounds are included, and what happens to the print plates if you reorder next season.

## Volume, timing and the IP terms

Most private label programmes start at one mixed container, with branded elements running alongside stock models. That keeps the entry point realistic while your branding goes live.

On IP: confirm who owns the design, what happens if the factory's ODM design is customised for you, and whether the supplier will keep your design off its own catalogue. A factory that hesitates on that last one is planning to sell it.
