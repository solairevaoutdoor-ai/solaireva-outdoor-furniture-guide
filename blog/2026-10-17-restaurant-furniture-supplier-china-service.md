# Restaurant Furniture Supplier China: Specifying for Daily Service

## Design for the reset, not the photo

A restaurant terrace is cleared, cleaned and reset between covers. That favours smooth powder-coated aluminum over timber, sealed cushions over loose ones, and tables without crevices where cutlery and glass collect.

Ask how many chairs stack in a metre. On a terrace stored nightly, four centimetres per chair multiplies fast, and stacking height is a purchasing line item most buyers never ask about.

## Density and wind

Terrace plans are getting tighter, so chairs are narrower and tables smaller and often rectangular. That changes the engineering: a narrower chair with the same load rating needs a different frame section, not just a smaller seat.

Then wind. Anything that moves has to be tied down or weighted, and on a listed building a heavier base can be cheaper and simpler than a fixing kit drilled into stone.

## Where the cost hides

Glass tops outdoors, untreated timber and light aluminum sections are the three most common regrets in commercial use. Coastal operators also move off 304 hardware after a season of corrosion rings around the fasteners.

The item gaining fastest is not furniture at all — it is shade. Operators spec umbrellas and pergolas with the seating so the colours match, which is easier when both come from one supplier.
