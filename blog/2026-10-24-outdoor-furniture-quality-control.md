# Outdoor Furniture Quality Control: The Four Stages That Matter

## Stage one: incoming material

Every quality problem that cannot be repaired starts here. Alloy number and profile section, timber moisture content, resin and UV additive on the weave, foam density, and the hardware grade on the incoming cartons.

None of these are visible in a finished chair, which is exactly why the incoming record matters. Ask to see it rather than a finished-goods photo.

## Stage two: in-line

In-line is where welding penetration, grinding finish, coating thickness at the joints, weave tension and tail finish are caught. A wrapped weave tail tucked back under a row lasts; a stapled one shows a rust line at the edge within a season.

Coating thickness is measured at the joint, not across the flat panel. Flat panels pass easily; joints and edges are where corrosion starts.

## Stage three and four: pre-packing and loading

Pre-packing inspection is the function test — wobble, load, hardware torque, and a full cycle on anything that reclines or folds, plus a comparison against the approved sample. Loading checks the packing against the sample and photographs the container.

The single number that summarises all four stages is the defect rate on shipped orders. Ours runs at 0.47%. Ask any supplier for theirs; the answer separates factories that measure from factories that hope.
