# Teak Outdoor Furniture Wholesale: Grades, Cost and What to Specify

## Grade is the whole conversation

Teak is one word on a quote and about six different materials in practice. Grade A heartwood from mature plantation trees is the commercial standard — dense, straight grain, fine for daily hotel use. Grade B carries more sapwood, and sapwood is the part that moves, greys and takes on water.

Reclaimed teak — old boats, bridges, buildings — photographs beautifully and is genuinely hard, but thickness varies board to board and you cannot reorder it. For a phased rollout where phase two must match phase one, reclaimed is a trap.

## What wholesale teak actually costs

Teak is priced per cubic metre rather than per chair, so the same dining table swings widely with grade and thickness. Expect roughly two to three times an equivalent powder-coated aluminum set, and the gap widens as the section thickens.

Freight is the second surprise. Teak is heavy, so a container of teak dining sets fills up on weight before it fills up on volume — the loading maths you use on aluminum does not transfer.

## What to write into the PO

Grade (A, B or kiln-dried reclaimed), origin, moisture content, thickness tolerance and the finish schedule — untreated, oiled or sealed. Ask whether the timber is FSC-certified and whether the mill supplies the legal-harvest documentation your market requires.

One line saves money later: spare slats and one spare table top per container. Teak is repaired rather than replaced, and matching timber two years on is the hardest part of a hotel order.
